A workflow is a **chain of agents** wired together so each one works on the previous one's output. Instead of one agent doing one job, a chain runs end to end, research feeding a writer, which feeds a publisher, all sharing one [Brain](/concepts/knowledge) and governed by your [decisions](/concepts/decisions).

<Note>
Workflows were previously called "automations" and built on the chains canvas. Same idea, now with a chat builder and a cleaner canvas.
</Note>

## Two ways to build one

- **Just describe it in chat.** At the bottom of the canvas, say what you want ("every Monday find 20 SaaS founders and email me a summary"). Orbit plans 1 to 5 mini agents, picks their tools, decides when it starts and where the result goes, and you press **Build it**.
- **Use the canvas.** Link agents yourself when you want fine control.

## The pieces

- **Start cards** fire the workflow: by hand, on a schedule, or from a webhook.
- **Mini agents** are the steps: one instruction each, a chosen set of tools, and a cap on tool calls.
- **End cards** send the result where it belongs: a Vault report, Telegram, email, or a signed webhook.

## Running and watching

- **A grid of every workflow** is the main view, with each schedule written in plain words.
- **Test runs** and a **run log** let you prove a chain works before you trust it.

## Why this is the unlock

Single agents are useful. Chained agents are a *company*. A lead-sourcing agent hands enriched leads to an outreach agent, a research agent hands findings to a content agent. Each step builds on the last, nothing is re-derived, and the whole chain reads from the same Brain and obeys the same decisions.
